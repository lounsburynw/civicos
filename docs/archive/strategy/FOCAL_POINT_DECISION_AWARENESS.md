# Focal Point: Decision Awareness

**Created**: Session 95 (November 2025)
**Status**: Pilot Strategy Document
**Priority**: CRITICAL - This defines what we're testing next

---

## TL;DR

**The Hypothesis**: Residents don't participate because they lack awareness of high-stakes decisions. If we detect decisions, find affected residents, and orchestrate coordination, they'll feel empowered to influence outcomes.

**The Pilot**: One decision, 5-10 residents, pre-meeting coordination. Measure: "Did this feel empowering?"

**Success = Expand. Failure = Pivot to alternative focal points.**

---

## The SeeClickFix Conundrum

### The Problem We Thought We Had

**Original Hypothesis (Sessions 89-93)**:
- San Rafael residents file operational complaints (potholes, stormwater)
- SeeClickFix handles them poorly (slow response, black holes)
- We bridge operational → policy engagement
- Residents get frustrated → join civic campaigns

**The Conundrum**:
What if San Rafael actually responds well to operational complaints?

**Then what's our value?**

### The Real Problem We Actually Have

**Even if operational channels work well, residents still face a critical gap:**

> **High-stakes decisions are made without your input because you didn't know about them.**

**Example: $1.1M Wildfire Prevention Fund (San Rafael, Oct 6, 2024)**

```
What Happened:
- City Council approved $1.1M wildfire prevention fund
- Only 12 residents testified
- Decision made, fund allocated, priorities set

What Should Have Happened:
- 10 residents who reported tree maintenance issues → notified
- 16 residents in high fire risk zones → notified
- Neighbors affected by wildfire policy → notified
→ 26 residents coordinate pre-meeting strategy
→ Aligned testimony with specific asks
→ Councilmember briefed on resident priorities
→ Stronger mandate for accountability
```

**The Gap**: Not operational responsiveness. **Decision awareness.**

## Four Focal Point Options (Evaluated Session 94)

### Option 1: Systemic Escalation
**Idea**: "Your pothole complaint is actually about inadequate preventive maintenance budget"

**Pros**:
- Educates residents about systemic issues
- Builds policy sophistication
- Aligns with SeeClickFix bridge strategy

**Cons**:
- Assumes operational channels fail (fragile)
- Requires resident to care about systemic policy (high bar)
- Hard to measure success (what's the outcome?)

**Verdict**: Too dependent on broken operational systems.

---

### Option 2: Decision Awareness ✅ SELECTED
**Idea**: "High-stakes decisions are happening. Here's who's affected. Let's coordinate."

**Pros**:
- Works regardless of operational responsiveness
- Clear, measurable pain point ("I didn't know")
- Testable hypothesis ("Did coordination feel empowering?")
- Foundation-friendly narrative (democratic participation)

**Cons**:
- Requires identifying "high-stakes" reliably
- Cold start: residents may not care even when informed
- Needs real decision coming up (timing dependent)

**Verdict**: Best balance of testability, measurability, and foundation appeal.

---

### Option 3: Accountability Tracking
**Idea**: "Council said they'd do X. Did they? We'll track it."

**Pros**:
- Clear value (hold government accountable)
- Differentiates from ChatGPT (longitudinal tracking)
- Builds trust through transparency

**Cons**:
- Requires successful coordination first (need campaigns to track)
- Very operationally intensive (human verification needed)
- Outcome tracking is downstream, not entry point

**Verdict**: Critical feature, but not the pilot focal point. Build after coordination works.

---

### Option 4: Legislative Routing
**Idea**: "This local decision connects to state bill SB-123. Here's how to influence both."

**Pros**:
- Leverages existing legislative enrichment infrastructure
- Multi-jurisdictional coordination (ambitious)
- Sophisticated policy engagement

**Cons**:
- High complexity for first pilot
- State legislature timing unpredictable
- Requires coordination to work locally first

**Verdict**: Phase 2 feature after Decision Awareness succeeds locally.

## Why Decision Awareness Wins

### 1. Testable Hypothesis

**The Claim**: "If residents knew about high-stakes decisions and could coordinate, they'd participate more effectively."

**The Test**:
- Find upcoming high-stakes decision (specific, measurable)
- Identify 5-10 affected residents
- Orchestrate pre-meeting coordination
- Measure: "Did you feel more capable of influencing the outcome?"

**Success Criteria**:
- >50% say yes → Expand (more decisions, more residents)
- <50% say no → Pivot (maybe they don't care, maybe coordination didn't help)

**This is falsifiable. You can run the experiment in 2-3 weeks.**

### 2. Foundation-Friendly Narrative

**Foundation Funders Care About**:
- Democratic participation (more voices heard)
- Equitable access (who currently has voice vs. who should)
- Measurable outcomes (did coordination increase participation?)
- Replicability (can this scale across cities?)

**Decision Awareness Checks All Boxes**:
- More residents participate in high-stakes decisions ✅
- Operational complaint data surfaces marginalized voices ✅
- Testimony count, resident empowerment (measurable) ✅
- Model works anywhere with agenda data + complaints ✅

**Accountability tracking and legislative routing are impressive, but Decision Awareness is the clearest foundation pitch.**

### 3. Works Regardless of Operational Responsiveness

**If SeeClickFix works well** (San Rafael responds fast):
- Great! Residents trust government
- But they still don't know about policy decisions
- We provide decision awareness, not complaint fixes

**If SeeClickFix works poorly** (some cities slow to respond):
- Frustration → operational → policy bridge still works
- Decision awareness becomes even more valuable (residents want power)

**Decision Awareness is robust to operational channel quality.**

### 4. Clear Entry Point for Pilot

**You can start tomorrow**:
1. Check San Rafael City Council calendar
2. Find next high-stakes decision (budget allocation, development, policy change)
3. Identify affected residents (SeeClickFix complaints, geography, follows)
4. Invite to pre-meeting strategy session
5. Coordinate testimony
6. Survey: "Did this feel empowering?"

**No new technology needed. Intelligence layer exists. Just orchestrate.**

## Pilot Design: Decision Awareness Validation

### Step 1: Identify High-Stakes Decision

**Criteria for "High-Stakes"**:
- Budget allocation >$500K (financial materiality)
- OR Development project >50 units (community impact)
- OR Policy change affecting >1,000 residents (broad impact)
- OR Environmental decision (wildfire, climate, water)

**San Rafael Upcoming Decisions to Check** (Session 96):
- City Council agendas (next 30 days)
- Planning Commission agendas (next 30 days)
- Budget meetings (if scheduled)

**Selection Criteria**:
- Must have clear affected population (geographic or complaint-based)
- Must have 7+ days lead time (time to coordinate)
- Must have potential for resident testimony
- Prefer first-time decision (not ongoing debate)

**Example Ideal Decision**:
- "$800K street repaving program" → 50+ pothole complaints
- "Approve 100-unit development at X address" → neighbors within 500m
- "Climate action plan funding" → residents who reported environmental issues

### Step 2: Identify Affected Residents (5-10 Target)

**Data Sources**:
1. **SeeClickFix complaints** (operational issues related to decision)
2. **Geographic proximity** (address within impact radius)
3. **Issue follows** (users tracking related topics on platform)
4. **Past participation** (testified on similar issues before)

**Outreach Method**:
- Email: "High-stakes decision affecting your neighborhood. Want to coordinate?"
- In-app notification: "New decision matched to your complaint"
- Text message (if available): "City Council voting on [X] - interested?"

**Goal**: 5-10 residents commit to pre-meeting coordination

**Fallback**: If <5 commit, document why (didn't care? bad timing? messaging unclear?)

### Step 3: Pre-Meeting Strategy Session

**Format**: Virtual meeting (Zoom/Google Meet), 60 minutes, 3-7 days before council meeting

**Agenda**:
1. **Context** (15 min): What's the decision? Why does it matter?
2. **Legislative background** (10 min): State bills, federal programs, budget context
3. **Strategy** (20 min): What do we want? Who testifies on what?
4. **Drafting** (10 min): Aligned talking points (not repetitive)
5. **Logistics** (5 min): Meeting time, sign-up process, follow-up

**Facilitation**: Light-touch (not scripting testimony, enabling coordination)

**Deliverable**: 5-10 residents feel prepared to testify in coordinated way

### Step 4: Meeting Participation

**Day Of**:
- Reminder notifications (morning of meeting)
- Coordination chat active (real-time adjustments)
- Optional: Someone attends to document (not orchestrate)

**What We're NOT Doing**:
- ❌ Pressuring residents to testify (voluntary participation)
- ❌ Scripting exact words (authentic voice matters)
- ❌ Political advocacy (we're coordination infrastructure, not activists)

**What We ARE Doing**:
- ✅ Making sure residents know the decision exists
- ✅ Providing context so testimony is informed
- ✅ Coordinating so testimony is aligned, not redundant
- ✅ Tracking whether coordination felt empowering

### Step 5: Impact Measurement

**Immediate Survey** (within 24 hours of meeting):

1. **Empowerment** (Primary metric):
   - "Did this coordination make you feel more capable of influencing the outcome?"
   - Scale: 1-5 (strongly disagree → strongly agree)
   - Target: >3.5 average

2. **Coordination Value**:
   - "Would you have participated without the coordination?"
   - Yes / Maybe / No
   - Target: >50% say "No" or "Maybe"

3. **Decision Awareness**:
   - "How did you learn about this decision?"
   - SeeClickFix match / Platform notification / Other
   - Goal: Understand discovery pathway

4. **Process Feedback**:
   - "What was most valuable about the coordination?"
   - Open text
   - Goal: Qualitative insights for iteration

**Follow-Up Survey** (30 days later):

5. **Outcome Awareness**:
   - "Do you know what the Council decided?"
   - Yes / No
   - Goal: Test accountability tracking need

6. **Continued Engagement**:
   - "Would you coordinate on future decisions?"
   - Yes / No / Depends
   - Target: >70% say "Yes"

**Qualitative Interviews** (Optional, 2-3 residents):
- 15-minute conversation
- "Tell me about the experience"
- "What would make this more valuable?"
- "Would you recommend this to neighbors?"

## Success Criteria

### Pilot Success = Expand
**If >50% of residents report feeling empowered:**
- Expand to more decisions (5-10 per month)
- Expand to more residents per decision (10-30)
- Add advocacy org coordination (Phase 2)
- Document case studies for foundation pitch

**Next Questions**:
- How many decisions per month can we orchestrate?
- What's the optimal resident-to-decision ratio?
- When should we involve advocacy orgs vs. resident-only?
- How do we measure influence on actual outcomes?

### Pilot Failure = Pivot
**If <50% report feeling empowered:**
- Interview to understand why:
  - Didn't care about the decision? (selection error)
  - Coordination didn't help? (process error)
  - Outcome felt predetermined? (systemic problem)
  - Too much effort for perceived impact? (value error)

**Potential Pivots**:
1. **Different focal point**: Try Accountability Tracking or Legislative Routing
2. **Different decisions**: Maybe high-stakes framing is wrong (try hyperlocal?)
3. **Different orchestration**: Maybe pre-meeting sessions are wrong format
4. **Fundamental rethink**: Maybe coordination isn't the right moat

**The pilot is designed to fail fast if the hypothesis is wrong.**

## Risk Mitigation

### Risk 1: Can't Find High-Stakes Decision in Next 30 Days
**Likelihood**: Medium (San Rafael meets 2x/month, not all items high-stakes)

**Mitigation**:
- Expand search to Planning Commission (more frequent)
- Expand timeframe to 60 days (more runway)
- Consider "medium-stakes" decision if no high-stakes available
- Fallback: Use Marin County decision (larger jurisdiction)

**Impact if occurs**: Delays pilot by 2-4 weeks (annoying but not fatal)

### Risk 2: <5 Residents Commit to Coordination
**Likelihood**: Medium (cold outreach, unfamiliar platform, time commitment)

**Mitigation**:
- Choose decision with clear affected population (50+ potential residents)
- Multiple outreach channels (email, SMS, in-app)
- Emphasize low time commitment (60-minute session, optional testimony)
- Offer incentive (not money, but "be part of civic pilot")

**Impact if occurs**: Reveals messaging problem OR selection problem (learn fast, pivot)

### Risk 3: Residents Coordinate But Feel Unempowered
**Likelihood**: Low-Medium (coordination should help, but outcome may feel predetermined)

**Mitigation**:
- Choose decision where testimony could plausibly influence (not done deal)
- Set expectations: "Coordination increases chance, doesn't guarantee outcome"
- Focus empowerment metric on "capable of influencing" not "we won"

**Impact if occurs**: Core hypothesis invalidated → Pivot to accountability tracking or legislative routing

### Risk 4: Council Decision Postponed/Canceled
**Likelihood**: Low (but possible - meeting reschedules, item pulled from agenda)

**Mitigation**:
- Identify 2-3 candidate decisions (backup options)
- Monitor agenda updates daily (week before meeting)
- Pivot to backup decision if primary cancels

**Impact if occurs**: Wastes 1 week of coordination (residents frustrated but documented learning)

### Risk 5: Residents Coordinate But Council Ignores Testimony
**Likelihood**: Medium (councils sometimes have predetermined votes)

**Mitigation**:
- This is actually a valuable learning (reveals accountability gap)
- Empowerment metric is about "capable of influencing" not "we won"
- Sets up natural transition to accountability tracking (Phase 2)

**Impact if occurs**: Validates need for outcome tracking, doesn't invalidate coordination value

## Key Decisions to Make (Session 96)

### 1. Which Decision to Test?

**Criteria**:
- High-stakes (budget/$500K+, development/50+ units, or broad policy)
- Clear affected population (geographic or complaint-based)
- 7-30 day lead time (time to coordinate)
- Testimony opportunity (public comment allowed)

**Evaluation Process**:
- List 3-5 candidate decisions
- Score each on criteria above
- Check affected resident count (need 20+ potential, get 5-10 actual)
- Choose highest scoring decision with sufficient affected population

**Document Selection Rationale**: Why this decision over alternatives?

### 2. How to Identify Affected Residents?

**Primary Method**:
- SeeClickFix complaints (if operational link exists)
- Geographic proximity (within 0.5 mile radius)
- Issue follows (users tracking related topics)

**Outreach Strategy**:
- Email (personalized, explains why they're affected)
- In-app notification (if user active on platform)
- SMS (if available and opted-in)

**Messaging**:
- Emphasize "You're affected by upcoming decision"
- Low time commitment (60-minute strategy session, optional testimony)
- Frame as pilot (helping build civic infrastructure)

### 3. How to Measure Empowerment?

**Primary Metric**: "Did coordination make you feel more capable of influencing the outcome?"
- Scale: 1-5
- Target: >3.5 average
- Threshold: >50% rate 4 or 5 (success)

**Secondary Metrics**:
- Would you coordinate again? (Yes/No)
- Would you recommend to neighbor? (Yes/No)
- What was most valuable? (Open text)

**Timing**: Survey within 24 hours of meeting (while experience is fresh)

## What Failure Looks Like (And How We Learn)

### Failure Mode 1: No One Cares
**Signal**: <5 residents commit despite 50+ affected

**Learning**: Decision awareness isn't the pain point (people don't care even when informed)

**Pivot**: Maybe the problem is outcome tracking (they've tried before, nothing changed) or legislative routing (local decisions feel powerless)

### Failure Mode 2: Coordination Doesn't Help
**Signal**: Residents participate but <50% feel empowered

**Learning**: Pre-meeting strategy sessions don't add value (format wrong? timing wrong? content wrong?)

**Pivot**: Different orchestration format (async coordination? day-of only? expert facilitation?)

### Failure Mode 3: Process Too Heavy
**Signal**: Residents interested but drop off before meeting

**Learning**: 60-minute strategy session is too much commitment

**Pivot**: Lighter-touch coordination (shared Google Doc? 15-minute briefing? AI-generated talking points only?)

### Failure Mode 4: Outcome Feels Predetermined
**Signal**: Residents testify but Council ignores (predetermined vote)

**Learning**: Coordination is necessary but not sufficient (accountability gap)

**Pivot**: Shift focus to accountability tracking + political champion engagement (need officials to amplify resident voice)

**The pilot is designed to surface these failure modes fast. 2-3 weeks, not 2-3 months.**
