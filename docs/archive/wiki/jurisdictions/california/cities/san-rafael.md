## Decision Timing (AI-Critical)

### Planning & Development
- **Planning applications**: 30-90 days average processing (varies by complexity)
- **Zoning changes**: Planning Commission review → City Council (2-4 months total)
- **Major projects (like Northgate)**: 2-3+ years with extensive community input
- **CEQA environmental review**: 6-12 months for projects requiring full EIR
- **Appeal periods**: 10 days after Planning Commission decisions

### Budget Process
- **Budget season**: February-June annually
- **Public input deadline**: May budget hearings for following fiscal year (July-June)
- **Department presentations**: April-May  
- **Final adoption**: June before July 1 fiscal year start

### Meeting Schedules
- **City Council**: 1st and 3rd Monday, 6:00 PM (Tuesdays if Monday holiday)
- **Planning Commission**: 2nd and 4th Tuesday, 7:00 PM
- **Study sessions**: Periodic, best opportunity for detailed input
- **Budget meetings**: Special schedule April-June

### Agenda Deadlines
- **Agenda posting**: 72 hours before meetings (Brown Act requirement)
- **Public comment submission**: Email by Monday noon for Tuesday Council meetings
- **Written testimony**: Submit to City Clerk for inclusion in record

## Success Data (AI-Critical)

### Recent Examples of Effective Public Engagement

1. **Northgate Town Square Redevelopment (2022-2024)**
   - **Strategy**: Developer conducted 40+ community meetings, advocates provided detailed alternatives
   - **Timing**: Multi-year engagement with revisions based on ongoing feedback
   - **Outcome**: City Council unanimous approval Dec 2024 with significant project modifications
   - **Key factor**: Sustained, organized community input through Sustainable San Rafael & MEHC

2. **Housing Element 2023-2031 (2022-2023)**
   - **Strategy**: Housing Element Working Group participation + detailed written comments
   - **Timing**: Early engagement during draft phase, follow-up during HCD review
   - **Outcome**: Successful HCD certification with community-requested refinements
   - **Contact method**: Formal letters to Council + working group participation

3. **1515 4th Street Housing Project (2023)**
   - **Strategy**: Sustainable San Rafael provided detailed design and affordability recommendations  
   - **Timing**: Early project review phase before final approvals
   - **Outcome**: City implemented improved design standards and workforce housing requirements
   - **Approach**: Technical expertise combined with specific policy recommendations

### Engagement Effectiveness Data
- **Email contact**: High response rate for specific, actionable requests to individual council members
- **Planning Commission testimony**: Most effective for technical/design issues before Council review
- **Study sessions**: Best format for detailed policy discussions and relationship building  
- **Written testimony**: Essential for complex issues, provides permanent record
- **Coalition building**: Organizations like Sustainable San Rafael and MEHC highly effective

### Political Dynamics
- **Mayor Kate Colin**: Strong housing advocate, responsive to sustainability concerns

- **Rachel Kertz**: Environmental sustainability and climate action priority
- **Planning Commission**: Technical review body, responsive to design and policy details

## Jurisdictional Authority 

### San Rafael City Boundaries (Incorporated Area)
- **Geographic Coverage**: San Rafael city limits (incorporated area only)
- **Population**: ~59,000 residents within city boundaries
- **NOT COVERED**: Unincorporated areas (Lucas Valley, Santa Venetia, etc.) → Marin County jurisdiction

### City Exclusive Authority
- **Within city limits**: Zoning, building permits, local streets, city budget, police services
- **Planning Commission**: Land use applications, design review, zoning compliance within city
- **City Council**: Policy decisions, budget, ordinances, major development approvals for incorporated area

### Outside City Authority (Requires Other Agencies)
- **Water service**: Marin Municipal Water District (MMWD) - independent elected board
- **Fire services**: Marin County Fire Department - county jurisdiction
- **Unincorporated areas**: Marin County Community Development Agency handles zoning/permits
- **Regional issues**: Golden Gate Transit, TAM, ABAG coordination required
