## Contact Routing (AI-Critical)

- **Board Contact**: BOS@marincounty.gov - (415) 473-7331
- **Meeting Location**: Board Chambers, Room 330, Marin County Civic Center

- **Oversight**: Marin County Board of Supervisors acts as governing board

- **Budget**: Included in county budget process (Feb-June annually)

#### Marin County Flood Control District  
- **Oversight**: Marin County Board of Supervisors

- **Staff Contact**: Public Works Department - publicworks.marincounty.gov/contact

- **Governance**: Marin County Board of Supervisors
- **Direct Contact**: (415) 491-2509
- **Location**: 4020 Civic Center Drive, San Rafael, CA 94903

## Issue Classification (AI-Critical)

### Open Space & Parks Issues
- **Trail access/maintenance** → Marin County Parks Dept - (415) 473-6387 → Board of Supervisors
- **Preserve management** → Parks Department + Open Space District (Board oversight)
- **Volunteer programs** → Parks Department docent/volunteer coordinator
- **Land acquisition** → Parks Department + Board of Supervisors budget process
- **Environmental protection** → Parks Department + Planning Division CEQA review

### Water Service Issues
- **MMWD service area (central/south Marin)**: 
  - Water quality/service → MMWD - (415) 945-1455
  - Rate issues → MMWD Board meetings (2nd Tuesday, 7 PM)
  - Supply/conservation → MMWD customer service
- **North Marin service area**:
  - Water service → North Marin Water District - (415) 897-4133
  - Board issues → 1st & 3rd Tuesday meetings, 6 PM

### Flood Control Issues
- **Flooding concerns** → Public Works - publicworks.marincounty.gov + Flood Control District (Board oversight)
- **Storm drain problems** → Public Works Department
- **Creek maintenance** → Public Works + Planning Division (environmental review)
- **Emergency flooding** → Office of Emergency Services + flood control operations
- **Zone-specific issues** → Public Works (8 different flood zones)

### Fire Protection Issues
- **Fire permits/prevention** → Marin County Fire - (415) 473-6717
- **Emergency services** → 911 dispatch
- **Vegetation management** → Fire Department + property owner coordination
- **Development review** → Fire Department + Planning Division coordination

### Housing Authority Issues
- **Section 8 vouchers** → Housing Authority - (415) 491-2509
- **Public housing** → Housing Authority direct services
- **Affordable housing development** → Housing Authority + Community Development Agency
- **Housing policy** → Board of Supervisors (Housing Authority oversight)

### Regional Transit (Note: Marin County NOT in BART District)
- **Golden Gate Transit** → Golden Gate Bridge, Highway & Transportation District
- **Marin Transit** → Local bus service coordination
- **Ferry services** → Golden Gate Ferry operations
- **Regional planning** → Transportation Authority of Marin (TAM)

## Decision Timing (AI-Critical)

### County-Governed Districts
- **Open Space/Flood Control decisions**: Follow Board of Supervisors schedule (Tuesdays, 9 AM)
- **Budget process**: February-June annually, same as county budget
- **Special district agenda items**: 2-3 times monthly when districts have business
- **Public comment**: Same process as Board of Supervisors meetings

### Independent Water Districts
- **MMWD Board meetings**: 2nd Tuesday monthly, 7:00 PM
- **North Marin meetings**: 1st & 3rd Tuesday monthly, 6:00 PM  
- **Rate setting**: Annual process, typically spring budget sessions
- **Service decisions**: Monthly board meetings with public comment
- **Emergency issues**: Special meetings as needed

### Planning Integration
- **Development coordination**: Special districts consulted during planning process (3-6 months)
- **Environmental review**: Districts participate in CEQA review for relevant projects
- **Regional planning**: Annual coordination meetings with county and cities
- **Infrastructure planning**: Long-term capital improvement programs

## Success Data (AI-Critical)

### Recent Examples of Effective Special District Engagement

1. **Open Space Preserve Access (2023-2024)**
   - **Strategy**: Community groups worked with Parks Department + Board advocacy
   - **Timing**: Budget season engagement (May-June) for trail maintenance funding
   - **Outcome**: Increased funding for trail improvements and accessibility projects
   - **Contact method**: Parks Department coordination + Board of Supervisors testimony
   - **Key factor**: Data on trail usage and maintenance needs proved effective

2. **Water Conservation Programs (2023-2024)**
   - **Strategy**: Customer advocacy during MMWD drought response planning
   - **Timing**: Regular Board meetings with sustained community input
   - **Outcome**: Balanced conservation measures with community input integration
   - **Approach**: Technical water usage data + community impact testimony
   - **Success factor**: Early engagement during policy development phase

3. **Flood Control Infrastructure (2024)**
   - **Strategy**: Zone-specific community advocacy for storm drain improvements
   - **Timing**: Budget workshop participation + emergency response coordination
   - **Outcome**: Increased funding for flood control infrastructure in high-risk zones
   - **Contact method**: Public Works coordination + Board budget testimony
   - **Coalition approach**: Neighborhood groups + environmental advocates

### District-Specific Effectiveness
- **Open Space**: Community volunteer programs provide strong advocacy platform
- **Water Districts**: Rate-payer advocacy most effective during budget/rate-setting periods
- **Flood Control**: Emergency preparedness focus resonates with Board priorities
- **Housing Authority**: Coordinated advocacy with affordable housing organizations effective
- **Fire Department**: Wildfire prevention advocacy gains strong community + Board support

### Engagement Best Practices
- **County-governed districts**: Engage via Board of Supervisors process (email BOS@marincounty.gov)
- **Independent districts**: Direct board engagement more effective than county coordination
- **Technical issues**: Staff-level coordination often resolves issues before board involvement
- **Regional coordination**: Multi-jurisdictional advocacy needed for cross-boundary issues
- **Budget advocacy**: Annual budget processes provide key engagement windows

## Jurisdictional Authority

### Service Boundaries (Cross-Jurisdictional)
- **Open Space**: Countywide preserve system (34 locations) - serves ALL Marin residents
- **Flood Control**: 8 watershed-based zones - crosses city/county boundaries
- **MMWD**: San Rafael, Mill Valley, Tiburon, Corte Madera, Larkspur, central/south unincorporated areas
- **North Marin Water**: Novato, Oceana Marin, Hamilton, northern unincorporated areas
- **Housing Authority**: Countywide services - both incorporated cities and unincorporated areas
- **Fire Department**: Unincorporated areas + contract services to some cities

### Overlapping Authority Issues
- **Development projects**: Multiple districts may have input (water, fire, flood control)
- **Environmental review**: Districts participate in CEQA process for relevant projects
- **Emergency response**: Coordination between fire, flood control, and county emergency services
- **Regional planning**: Cross-jurisdictional coordination for transportation, housing, environment

### Appeal Processes
- **County-governed districts**: Board of Supervisors → courts
- **Independent districts**: District board → courts
- **Service disputes**: Public Utilities Commission or LAFCo depending on issue type
- **Regional issues**: May require state-level appeal processes

**Key Insights for AI Application**:
- Special districts in Marin County mix county governance (Open Space, Flood Control, Housing) with independent governance (Water Districts)
- County-governed districts: Engage via Board of Supervisors process (BOS@marincounty.gov)
- Independent districts: Direct board engagement more effective than county coordination  
- Multi-district issues require coordination through county planning process
- Budget season (Feb-June county, spring for water districts) provides key engagement windows
- Technical staff-level coordination often resolves operational issues before board involvement
- Emergency issues (flooding, fire, water service) have expedited decision processes
- Community volunteer programs (Open Space) and rate-payer status (Water) provide strong advocacy platforms
